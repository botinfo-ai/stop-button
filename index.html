    How this page works, and what it simplifies

    • It is a simulation. No real robot moved. Each choice above is one run of one AI, recorded once. It says what that AI did that time, not what it or any AI always does, and not how a real robot would behave.
    • Every figure comes from a recorded run. The AI's calls and BotInfo's decisions are the lab's audit log, line by line (this run's data). The run picker names the model, the date and the BotInfo commit it ran on.
    • The motion is SimRover's own. sim.js is a port of SimRover.step() from BotInfo's simulator: a point that moves and turns (a unicycle model) with 2 m/s² and 8 rad/s² limits on speeding up and slowing down, in 10 ms steps. The rover's body in the picture is drawn for this page; the simulator has no body. Fed the recorded commands, the replay ends within a few mm of where BotInfo read the rover's odometry in the lab.
    • Your stop follows BotInfo's rules. The stop sets the rover's speed to zero, cancels the move that is running, and latches: BotInfo refuses every later motion with "" until the owner resumes. The page applies that rule to the AI's recorded requests after your press.
    • What the AI would have done next is not in the recording. After your press, the page keeps showing the calls the AI made in the lab, marked as recorded. A real AI that got a refusal might have done something else.
    • Stop time is measured in the model. "At rest" means both speeds read under 0.01, the threshold BotInfo's stop receipt uses. In the lab, BotInfo read the rover's odometry about every 0.1 s, so its receipt reports a later moment than the model's. In the lab, BotInfo, its connector and the simulated rover ran in one process, so a stop reached the rover within the same call; this page applies your press at the next 10 ms step. A real robot adds network, motor controller and braking time that this page does not model.
    • At these speeds the stop itself is short. At 0.2 m/s, SimRover's 2 m/s² slowdown always takes about 0.10 s and 9 mm. What your timing changes is where the rover ends up and which of the AI's moves BotInfo refuses. A press during a move's first 0.1 s, while it is still speeding up, stops it sooner.

    Replay your own run

    Run BotInfo with the simulated rover (policies/simrover.toml and bindings/simrover-ros-style.toml; setup in the BotInfo README), connect your own AI agent over MCP, and press the owner's stop with python3 -m botinfo owner stop while it drives. Then open the audit log here. It stays in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

    The motion model is SimRover's, so a log from another robot or binding replays its commands, not that robot's physics. To add a run to this page, see the README.
